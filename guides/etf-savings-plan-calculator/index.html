ETF savings plan calculator: how compound interest grows your money

A monthly ETF savings plan is one of the simplest ways to start investing: you put in a fixed amount every month, say 100 €, and let it run. But what does that actually add up to? At an assumed 7 % a year, 100 € a month grows to about 52,400 € after 20 years and about 122,700 € after 30 years, although you only paid in 36,000 € over those 30 years.

This guide explains how compound interest works, how to calculate a savings plan, how much you need to save for a goal, and why time, fees and inflation make such a big difference. All numbers are examples based on assumed returns, because nobody can predict the stock market.

Compound Interest & Portfolio

What compound interest means

With compound interest, it is not only your contributions that grow but also the returns you have already earned. With ETFs these are price gains and dividends rather than interest in the strict sense, but the principle is the same: if 1,000 € grows by 7 %, you have 1,070 €. Next year, 1,070 € grows, not 1,000 €.

At first the effect looks small. Over many years, though, it becomes the largest part of your wealth: at some point your returns contribute more than your own deposits.

How a savings plan is calculated

In a savings plan, new money comes in every month. A common way to calculate it: the contribution is invested at the start of the month, then the whole portfolio grows by one twelfth of the annual return. At 7 % a year, that is about 0.58 % a month. Repeat this for every month of the plan.

By hand that is tedious; a compound interest calculator does it in seconds. Owlvest calculates exactly this way, month by month, and shows your contributions and returns separately in a chart and a year-by-year table.

Time is the biggest lever

Compare 20 and 30 years at 7 %: with 100 € a month you reach about 52,397 € after 20 years and about 122,709 € after 30 years. The extra ten years cost you 12,000 € in contributions but add more than 70,000 €.

If you start ten years later, doubling your contribution is not enough to catch up: 200 € a month at 7 % for 20 years grows to about 104,793 €, still less than 100 € a month over 30 years. Starting early, even with small amounts, pays off.

How much to save for 100,000 € or 1 million

You can also turn the question around: how much do you need to put in each month to reach a target? Same assumptions as above (contribution at the start of each month, constant return, no fees, taxes or inflation), rounded to whole euros:

  • 100,000 € in 20 years: about 191 € a month at 7 %, or 242 € at 5 %
  • 100,000 € in 30 years: about 81 € a month at 7 %, or 120 € at 5 %
  • 1 million € in 30 years: about 815 € a month at 7 %, or 1,197 € at 5 %
  • 1 million € in 40 years: about 379 € a month at 7 %, or 653 € at 5 %

Fees: the TER adds up

Every ETF has ongoing costs, stated as the TER (total expense ratio) per year. They are taken directly from the fund's assets and reduce your return every year. Over decades this adds up noticeably, because fees compound too.

Same example, 100 € a month for 30 years at an assumed 7 % return before fees:

Inflation: what your money is worth later

122,709 € in 30 years is not the same as 122,709 € today. With an assumed inflation rate of 2 % a year, the final value from the 7 % example equals only about 67,744 € in today's purchasing power. At a 5 % return it would be about 46,138 €.

That does not mean saving is pointless; money left in a checking account loses value to inflation just the same. But it helps you set realistic goals. That is why Owlvest also shows your result in today's money.

Why your real result will differ

A calculator with a fixed return pretends your ETF grows evenly every year. In reality, stock prices swing a lot: years with gains of more than 20 % can be followed by years with significant losses. The 7 % in the example is an assumed average, not a promise.

For a savings plan, the order of returns matters too. A crash shortly before the end hits a large portfolio harder than a crash in the early years, when you are still buying cheaply. So it makes sense to look at a range rather than a single number. Owlvest Premium simulates many possible market paths and shows a pessimistic, a middle and an optimistic outcome.

Don't forget taxes

The examples above are before taxes. How returns are taxed depends on your country. In Germany, for example, gains are subject to a flat withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge, reduced by a 30 % partial exemption for equity ETFs and an annual saver's allowance, and accumulating ETFs are also taxed a little every year through the Vorabpauschale, explained in our guide to the Vorabpauschale.

Frequently asked questions

What return should I assume for an ETF savings plan?

Nobody knows for sure. Broadly diversified stock ETFs have often returned more than 5 % a year over long periods in the past, but past returns guarantee nothing. Run the numbers with several assumptions, such as 5 % and 7 %, and plan with the more cautious one.

How much will 200 € a month be worth in 20 years?

At an assumed constant 7 % a year, about 104,793 € before fees, taxes and inflation, of which 48,000 € is your own money. At 5 % it would be about 82,550 €.

How much do I need to save a month to reach 100,000 €?

At an assumed 7 % a year, about 191 € a month for 20 years or about 81 € a month for 30 years. With a lower return or fees, you need more.

How accurate is a compound interest calculator for ETFs?

It shows what you would get with a steady return. Because prices fluctuate, your actual result will differ, up or down. It is a planning tool, not a forecast.

Is 50 or 100 € a month worth it?

Yes, especially over long periods. In the example, 100 € a month at an assumed 7 % grows to about 122,709 € after 30 years, of which 36,000 € is paid in. Starting early and sticking with it often matters more than the amount.
