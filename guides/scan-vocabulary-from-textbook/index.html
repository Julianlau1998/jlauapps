How to scan vocabulary from a textbook into flashcards

Typing a vocabulary list into flashcards by hand takes longer than learning the first half of it. Text recognition on the phone has become good enough that you can photograph the page instead and spend your time on the words themselves.

The quality of the result still depends a lot on the photo. This guide covers how to take a picture that scans cleanly, what to check before saving, and how to organize the cards so they stay useful until the exam.

Take a photo that scans well

Text recognition works best when the page looks as close as possible to a clean, flat document. A few habits make a big difference.

  • Use even light. Daylight from a window or a ceiling lamp works well; avoid your own shadow falling across the page.
  • Watch for glare. Glossy textbook paper reflects light, so tilt the phone slightly if you see a bright spot over the words.
  • Flatten the page. Press the book open so lines near the spine aren’t curved, or hold the edge down with one hand.
  • Hold the phone parallel to the page and fill the frame with the vocabulary area, not the whole double page.
  • Keep it sharp. Tap to focus and hold still for a moment; blurry letters are the most common reason for errors.
  • One list per photo. If a page mixes a vocabulary box with exercises, frame just the box.

Columns, tables and tilted photos

Most textbooks print vocabulary in two or three columns: the word, sometimes an example or phonetic spelling, and the translation. The tricky part for any scanner is matching each word to the right translation, especially when an entry wraps onto a second line.

Cardfox is built for this layout. According to its description, it recognizes each word and its translation, even in columns and on tilted photos, and you can also pick an existing photo or open a PDF instead of using the camera. Even so, a straight, well-lit photo gives any recognition the best chance.

Check the results before saving

No scanner is perfect, so take a minute to review the list before it turns into cards. It’s much faster to fix an entry now than to stumble over a wrong card in three weeks.

  • Compare a few entries with the book, especially long phrases and words with accents or umlauts.
  • Deselect anything you don’t want to learn, such as headings, page numbers or words you already know.
  • Check which side is which. If the foreign word ended up on the back, swap sides.
  • Keep articles and plural forms with nouns if your teacher expects them, for example “der Hund, die Hunde”.

Organize decks by unit

A deck per textbook unit (or chapter) keeps things easy to find and matches how most school tests are set: “Unit 4 vocabulary on Thursday.” You can still study all units together later, but separate decks let you focus on what’s coming up.

Give each deck a clear name like “English – Unit 4” or “Spanish B1 – Lección 7”. If you learn several subjects, colors help you tell them apart at a glance. In Cardfox, decks can have their own color, so one color per subject works well.

Plan toward the exam date

The biggest mistake is scanning the whole unit the night before. Spaced repetition needs a few days to work, because each word should come back several times at growing intervals.

  • Scan the new unit as soon as you start it in class, not when the test is announced.
  • Study a little every day instead of one long session.
  • Learn in both directions for words you need to write, not just recognize.
  • In the last days, focus on the words you keep getting wrong.

Frequently asked questions

Can I scan handwritten vocabulary lists?

Text recognition handles clear handwriting better than it used to, but printed text is far more reliable. If you scan handwritten notes, write neatly, leave space between word and translation, and check every entry before saving.

Can I just use Live Text on my iPhone?

On supported iPhones, Live Text lets you select and copy text from a photo or the camera view. That works for a few words, but you still have to pair each word with its translation by hand. A flashcard scanner that detects word–translation pairs saves that step.

Can I make flashcards from a PDF?

Yes, if the app supports it. In Cardfox you can open a PDF instead of taking a photo, for example a worksheet from your teacher. As with photos, check the detected pairs before saving.

Should I scan the whole double page?

Usually not. Frame just the vocabulary area. Exercises, headings and images around it only add entries you have to deselect later.

What if I don’t have the textbook with me?

Cardfox can suggest vocabulary for any language, level (A1 to C1) and topic. You can also add words from any app via the share sheet with “Add to Cardfox”, or type cards yourself.

How far ahead of an exam should I start?

As early as possible, ideally when the unit starts in class. A week gives spaced repetition enough room for several reviews of each word; the night before does not.
