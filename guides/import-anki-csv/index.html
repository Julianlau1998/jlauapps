Moving flashcards between apps: CSV and Anki text files

Flashcards you have typed or collected over months are worth keeping, even if you switch apps, devices or study tools. The most reliable way to move them is also the oldest: a plain text file where each line is one card.

This guide explains how CSV and tab-separated files for flashcards are built, how to get your notes out of Anki as plain text, and which small details (separators, quotes, encoding) cause most failed imports.

What a flashcard file looks like

A flashcard export is just a table saved as text. Each line is one card, and a separator character splits the line into columns: front, back, and sometimes tags.

CSV stands for comma-separated values, but in practice “CSV” files also use semicolons or tabs. A tab-separated file is often called TSV and frequently ends in .txt or .tsv. A minimal example with a semicolon as separator looks like the lines below. The first line is an optional header; many apps recognize common column names like Front and Back and skip that line instead of turning it into a card.

  • Front;Back
  • the dog;der Hund
  • to run;laufen
  • It’s raining.;Es regnet.

Separators, quotes and line breaks

Problems start when the content itself contains the separator. “to take, took, taken” has two commas, so in a comma-separated file it would be split into three columns. The standard solution is quoting: wrap the field in double quotes, and write a double quote inside a quoted field as two double quotes.

  • Tabs are the safest separator for vocabulary, because they almost never appear inside a word or sentence.
  • Semicolons are a good second choice for language cards; commas are the most likely to appear inside content.
  • Keep it to one card per line unless the field is properly quoted.

Encoding: why umlauts turn into strange characters

Text files store characters as bytes, and the encoding defines how. Today’s standard is UTF-8, which covers every language, from ä and ñ to Japanese and Arabic. If a file was saved in an older encoding such as Windows-1252 and opened as UTF-8 (or the other way around), “Mädchen” can turn into something like “MÃ¤dchen”.

When you create a CSV in a spreadsheet, choose the UTF-8 option if there is one. Recent versions of Excel offer “CSV UTF-8 (Comma delimited)” as a separate format from plain “CSV”. Also note that Excel with German regional settings uses semicolons instead of commas in CSV files, which is fine as long as the importing app recognizes the separator.

Exporting plain text from Anki

Anki can export notes as a tab-separated text file. In the desktop version, go to File > Export, pick the deck you want, and choose “Notes in Plain Text” as the format. Depending on your version you will see options such as including HTML and media references or including tags.

Some things to know about this format:

  • It exports note fields as text. Images and audio files are not included; at most you get references to them, like [sound:word.mp3].
  • Review history and scheduling are not part of a plain text export. In the new app, the cards start as new.
  • If your notes have more than two fields (for example word, translation, example sentence), each field becomes its own column.
  • Unchecking the HTML option gives cleaner text if your cards contain formatting.

Importing and exporting with Cardfox

Cardfox, a vocabulary flashcard app for iPhone and iPad, can import and export cards as CSV or Anki text files. That means you can bring in a list you prepared in a spreadsheet or exported from Anki, and take your cards with you as a plain file at any time.

After importing, Cardfox schedules the cards with FSRS, a modern spaced repetition algorithm, so they come back just before you would forget them. Because plain text files don’t carry review history, imported cards start fresh there, as they would in any other app.

Bottom line

Two columns, a tab or semicolon as separator, UTF-8 encoding and quotes around anything tricky: that format moves between almost any flashcard tools. Keep an export of your decks now and then, and your work never depends on a single app. If you’re looking for a place to study those cards with spaced repetition, Cardfox reads exactly these files.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep my Anki review history when switching apps?

Not with a plain text export. “Notes in Plain Text” contains the card contents (and optionally tags), not scheduling data, so imported cards start as new.

Should I use commas, semicolons or tabs?

Tabs are the least likely to clash with your content. Semicolons work well for most language cards. Commas are fine if fields containing commas are wrapped in double quotes.

Why does my CSV from Excel use semicolons?

Excel uses the list separator from your regional settings. In many European locales, including German, that is a semicolon, because the comma is the decimal separator.
